# Small Business Security Starter Kit

A free, plain-language Information Security Policy and adoption guide for small businesses, built around the [NIST Cybersecurity Framework](https://www.nist.gov/cyberframework).

Many small businesses have no written security policy, yet cyber insurers, larger clients, and vendors increasingly ask for one. This kit gives you a real starting point instead of a blank page.

## What's inside

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| [`Security_Starter_Kit.pdf`](Security_Starter_Kit.pdf) | The designed kit: why it matters, the full policy, how to adopt it, and what it does not cover |
| [`Information_Security_Policy.md`](Information_Security_Policy.md) | The same policy as editable text. Copy it into Word or Google Docs and fill in the brackets |

## The policy covers

- Access control
- Data protection
- Device and endpoint security
- Passwords and multi-factor authentication
- Network security
- Incident reporting
- Security awareness training
- Third-party and vendor management

## How to use it

1. Copy the policy and fill in the bracketed fields (business name, effective date, incident contact).
2. Change anything that doesn't match how your business actually works. A policy should describe what you really do.
3. Have whoever handles your IT confirm the technical statements are accurate.
4. Get sign-off from ownership and share it with your team.
5. Review it at least once a year, and whenever your tools, team, or business change.

## What this is not

- Not a substitute for a risk assessment of your own systems and data
- Not a guarantee of compliance with any regulation (HIPAA/PHIPA, PCI DSS, PIPEDA, GDPR, and others have their own requirements)
- Not legal advice

Industry-specific rules often apply on top of this template. If you handle patient records, payment cards, or other regulated data, get the policy tailored by someone who knows those requirements.
